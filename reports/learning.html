Lift telemetry · learning across workouts

How well it knows your lifting

Each workout's own report is about that workout. This page tracks the system itself: what it has learned about how you do each exercise, and how close it is to working from sensor data alone. It updates after every workout.

1 · Readiness

Sensor only, workout by workout

For every workout the log is hidden and the sensor-only mode finds the sets, names the exercises and counts the reps using only what it learned from other workouts. Readiness is the share of logged sets it gets fully right: found, named correctly, and with the exact rep count.

3 · Learning your lifting

What it has learned about how you lift

Every checked set teaches the system something about the exercise as you do it: which signal shows your reps most reliably, whether setup moves look like reps, your usual tempo and range of motion, and what the movement looks like to the sensor. A learned setting replaces the generic one only when evidence from at least workouts shows it doing better on sets it wasn't tuned on.

4 · Scoreboard

Every recorded algorithm change

One row per benchmark run over all checked workouts. A change is kept only if it doesn't make any workout worse.

5 · Across people

Checked against public data

Your workouts show how well it fits you; they can't show whether the methods are sound in general. So every algorithm change is also scored on the RecoFit public dataset (Microsoft, 2014): forearm-worn sensor recordings from many people with true rep counts. The published RecoFit system counted within ±1 rep 93% of the time; that's the bar.

6 · How it learns

The rules

  1. Checked workouts are the teacher. After each workout the set windows are checked against the recording and marked reviewed. Only those workouts feed learning and scoring.
  2. Never graded on its own homework. A workout's shadow test uses knowledge from the other workouts only, and learned settings are tested on sets they weren't tuned on.
  3. One day isn't enough. A learned setting needs evidence from at least workouts and a held-out win of at least reps per set over the generic setting. On the first workout alone, tuning made held-out counts worse, which is why this rule exists.
  4. Your logs are the training data. Keep logging exercise names and rep counts. Readiness should climb as workouts accumulate; when it stays high across several workouts, sensor data alone becomes enough.
