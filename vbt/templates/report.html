3 · Logged vs sensed

Did you miscount?

Each set gives two counts from the watch: all rep-like cycles, and the clean ones after dropping cycles whose depth or speed doesn't fit (nearly always unracking, a setup pull or re-racking). When the logged count lies between the two, log and watch agree. "Threshold range" is the all-cycles count as the detection threshold moves from 25% to 45% of a typical rep. For one-leg or one-arm exercises logged per side, each bout is compared separately. Click a row to open the set.

Can the watch name the exercise?

Sixteen motion features per set (orientation, rotation, translation). Within this session: leave-one-set-out, so each set is labelled by a model trained on the others. 10 · Finding the sets

How the sets were located

The detector reads the Hevy log in order and searches the recording for runs of regular reps that fit each logged set's rep count, with consistent wrist orientation and range of motion between sets of the same exercise and plausible rest gaps. It needs no hand marking. When a human has checked the windows, the report uses those and shows how close the detector came.

11 · Methods & caveats

How this was made, and what not to trust

Pipeline

    Caveats

    1. Wrist ≠ bar. The watch measures the forearm it's strapped to. On barbell presses and pulls the wrist follows the bar closely; on curls, extensions and raises it travels an arc, so "velocity" is the wrist's vertical speed, not the load's. Compare within an exercise, never across.
    2. One arm only. Unilateral and alternating work is only partly visible, and machines or bodyweight moves where the hands don't travel may not show up at all.
    3. Absolute velocities run a little low. Smoothing and zero-velocity resets bias mean velocity downward; trends within a set or ramp are more reliable than any single number.
    4. e1RM from a single day's ramp with an assumed minimum velocity threshold: read it as about ±10 kg.
    5. Small n. Within-session classifier numbers are optimistic until enough sessions build up for the across-session test.
    6. Turnaround ambiguity. Unracking, setup pulls and re-racking can look like reps, and fast touch-and-go reps can merge. Flagged reps are shown, not hidden.
